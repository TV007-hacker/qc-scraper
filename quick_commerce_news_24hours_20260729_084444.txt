SUMMARY Building materials quick commerce startup Fixxly has raised $5.5 Mn in a seed round from Accel, Fireside Ventures and Lightspeed India Partners Founded by DesignCafe cofounder Shezan Bhojani, along with Sachith Varma, the startup is looking to bring the quick commerce model to the building materials supply chain Fixxly will use the fresh capital to strengthen its technology platform ahead of its commercial launch in Bengaluru on September 1 FOLLOW US FOLLOW US Added to Saved Stories in Login Building materials quick commerce startup Fixxly has raised $5.5 Mn (₹52.6 Cr) in a seed funding round from Accel, Fireside Ventures and Lightspeed India Partners. The startup plans to use the fresh capital to strengthen its technology platform and scale operations ahead of its commercial launch on September 1, 2026. Fixxly will initially launch its services in Bengaluru before expanding to other cities. Founded in 2026 by Shezan Bhojani and Sachith Varma, Fixxly is building an AI-driven quick commerce platform for construction materials, including plywood, paint, electricals, lighting, hardware and plumbing supplies. The startup claims it will deliver these products to construction and home improvement sites within 30 minutes through a network of dark stores. Fixxly is targeting electricians, plumbers, carpenters, painters, civil contractors, interior designers, contractors and homeowners. It aims to address delays caused by the fragmented building materials procurement system, which is largely dependent on local dealers and typically designed for planned or next-day purchases. Fixxly said its AI-powered supply network will match orders with real-time inventory availability, customer proximity and local demand to fulfil urgent purchases. Prior to starting Fixxly, Bhojani cofounded home interior brand DesignCafe, which was acquired by HomeLane in 2024. The funding comes as India’s quick commerce sector expands well beyond its grocery roots. What started as a model for delivering daily essentials has steadily moved into categories such as electronics, beauty and personal care, fashion, medicines, home improvement, and construction materials, as startups look to address a wider range of urgent consumer and business needs. Driving this expansion is India’s quick commerce market, which is projected to grow to $68 Bn by 2031 from $8.3 Bn in 2026, at a compound annual growth rate of 52%. Investors are aggressively backing startups looking to disrupt sectors through quick commerce. For instance, Inc42 exclusively reported in March that HomeRun, which also operates in the construction and home improvement materials sector, is in talks to raise ₹100 Cr.

SUMMARY Vishal Mega Mart is ramping up its quick commerce push, expanding the service to 767 stores across 520 cities, with the platform crossing 1.4 Cr registered users The move signals that traditional retailers are increasingly embracing quick commerce as consumer buying habits shift, even as rivals like DMart opt against competing on ultra-fast deliveries Quick commerce now contributes 2%-9% of sales at Vishal Mega Mart stores where it is available, with most locations already generating around 5% of store revenue FOLLOW US FOLLOW US Added to Saved Stories in Login Amid growing penetration of quick commerce in Indian retail, value retail chain Vishal Mega Mart is looking to double down on quick deliveries. Speaking during Q1 earnings call, managing director and CEO Gunender Kapur said that the channel is now contributing meaningfully to store sales and helping it reach new customers as it expands beyond its traditional offline business. Vishal Mega Mart’s quick commerce network has expanded to 767 stores across 520 cities, with the number of registered users crossing 1.4 Cr. The retailer entered the quick commerce race in 2024, offering deliveries within 30 minutes. Quantifying quick commerce’s contribution to the legacy retail chain’s top line, Kapur said that the segment, although nascent, is currently contributing in a range 2-9% to a store revenue. “But in a majority of the places, the contribution is at least 5% to our store revenue now, which has been very encouraging for us because when we had started this journey, we had said that our goal would be 5% of store revenue. So in most places, we are achieving the 5% level. But in our best places, we are going up to 9% to 10%,” Kapur said. The comments come as quick commerce increasingly reshapes India’s retail landscape, prompting traditional retailers to rethink their growth strategies. While the segment has largely been led by players like Blinkit, Zepto and Swiggy Instamart, an increasing number of offline retailers are either integrating rapid deliveries into their existing store networks or adapting their business models in line with the shift in consumer behaviour. Unlike standalone quick commerce companies, Vishal Mega Mart fulfills orders through its existing stores, allowing it to leverage its physical footprint without investing in a separate dark store network. Kapur added that the average order value on quick commerce is around ₹800, slightly higher than offline shoppers. Further, he shared that 20% of quick commerce customers for Vishal Mega Mart have never shopped at its physical stores, making them entirely new customers for the brand. The strategy stands in contrast to DMart’s approach. While the retail major has acknowledged that quick commerce is becoming a permanent part of the retail ecosystem, it has ruled out competing on ultra-fast deliveries. Instead, it plans to focus on its Everyday Low Price (EDLP) proposition and scale DMart Ready by improving customer density, operating efficiencies and profitability rather than pursuing aggressive expansion or smaller delivery windows.
